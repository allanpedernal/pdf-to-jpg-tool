How to Convert PDF to JPG Without Losing Quality

The settings that actually matter when turning PDF pages into sharp JPG images — resolution, DPI, and compression explained in plain English.

PDF Tools Team · May 20, 2026 · 2 min read

Converting a PDF to JPG sounds simple — until the result comes out blurry, pixelated, or weirdly soft around the text. The good news is that quality loss is almost always caused by two or three settings you can control. This guide explains exactly what those settings do and how to keep your images sharp.

Why PDF-to-JPG conversion can lose quality

A PDF page is usually vector data (text and shapes described by math), so it stays crisp at any zoom level. A JPG is a raster image — a fixed grid of pixels. Converting means "rendering" the page into pixels at a chosen resolution, then compressing it. Two things decide the result:

  • Resolution / DPI — how many pixels the page is rendered into. Too few and everything looks soft.
  • JPEG compression — JPG throws away detail to shrink file size. Push it too hard and you get blocky "artifacts" around text and edges.

Pick the right resolution (DPI)

DPI (dots per inch) is the single biggest factor. As a rule of thumb:

  • 72–96 DPI — fine for on-screen thumbnails and previews.
  • 150 DPI — a good default for sharing or viewing on screens.
  • 300 DPI — print quality; use this when the JPG will be printed or zoomed.

If your tool offers a scale slider instead of a DPI box, higher scale = higher resolution. Doubling the scale roughly doubles the pixel dimensions (and the file size).

Set JPEG quality to 85–95%

JPEG quality is a trade-off between file size and fidelity. For documents with text, stay in the 85–95% range. Below ~80% you'll start to see fuzzy halos around letters. At 100% the file gets large with almost no visible benefit, so 90% is usually the sweet spot.

Tip: If sharp text matters more than file size — for example a scanned contract — consider exporting to PNG instead. PNG is lossless and never adds compression artifacts.

Step-by-step

  1. Open the PDF to JPG converter and drag your PDF onto the drop zone.
  2. Set the resolution to 150 DPI for screen use or 300 DPI for print.
  3. Set JPEG quality to around 90%.
  4. Convert, then check one page at 100% zoom before downloading the rest.

Because this tool runs entirely in your browser, your PDF is never uploaded to a server — the rendering happens on your own device, which is both private and fast.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my converted JPG look blurry?

Almost always low DPI. Re-export at 150 or 300 DPI and the text will sharpen up immediately.

Is JPG or PNG better for scanned documents?

PNG, if you want perfectly crisp text and don't mind a larger file. JPG is better when you need smaller files and the page contains photos.

Will converting reduce the original PDF's quality?

No. You're creating new image files; the original PDF is untouched.
