0. THE VERDICT — WHAT IS ACTUALLY WRONG
----------------------------------------------------------------
The rejection reason you were given translates to "Low value
content". You read that as "AdSense wants dynamic content, and my
site is static". That is not what it means, and chasing a dynamic
backend would waste months.

AdSense does not care whether HTML is generated at build time or
request time. Its reviewers fetch a URL and read the HTML that
comes back. "Dynamic content" in their feedback means *substantive,
regularly updated, original content that a human reader would find
valuable* — not server-side rendering.

Here is what their crawler actually receives today. I stripped
<script>, <style> and comments from each deployed page and counted
the remaining words:

TWENTY of your twenty-eight indexable pages — every single tool
page, the pages that ARE the product — serve 79 words and ZERO
headings. The entire tool UI, the H1, the how-to steps and the FAQ
are injected by JavaScript after load. To a reviewer skimming the
raw response, and to any crawler that does not execute your JS,
this site is twenty near-identical 79-word stubs wrapped around an
ad unit. That is a textbook "low value content" / "site does not
have enough original content" rejection, and it is also why the
tool pages are not ranking.

There is a second, sharper problem on the same pages. They emit
FAQPage and HowTo structured data describing questions and steps
that DO NOT EXIST in the served HTML. Google treats structured
data that is not visible to the user as a spam violation
("Marked-up content is not visible to users"). That can earn a
manual action on top of the AdSense rejection. Fix this before
reapplying, not after.

The good news: the fix already exists in your repo.
scripts/build-tool-pages.js contains the H1, intro, how-to and FAQ
copy for every tool (verified — the data table is populated for all
20 tools). The generated pages were never committed. This is a
build step that was never run, not content that needs writing.

DO NOT REAPPLY UNTIL PHASE 0 IS DONE. Every rejection makes the
next review stricter, and you cannot reapply immediately.

1. PHASE 0 — APPROVAL BLOCKERS (do these first, ~1 week)
----------------------------------------------------------------
B1. Regenerate every tool page with real, crawlable content.
    Run: node scripts/build-tool-pages.js
    Then verify, per page, that the SERVED HTML contains:
      - one <h1> matching the page's target keyword
      - a 150-250 word intro that is genuinely different per tool
        (not a template with the noun swapped — near-duplicate
        pages across 20 URLs is its own low-value signal)
      - a visible "How to..." step list
      - a visible FAQ whose questions match the FAQPage schema
        word for word
      - a related-tools block and 2-3 links to relevant blog posts
    Target: 600+ crawlable words per tool page, and no two pages
    sharing more than ~30% of their prose.
    Verify with: curl the live URL and count words with JS
    disabled. Not with the browser's rendered view — that is the
    mistake that hid this for three months.

B2. Reconcile structured data with visible content.
    After B1, every FAQPage question and HowTo step must appear as
    readable text on the page. If any schema block cannot be
    matched to visible copy, delete the schema block. Validate
    each template at search.google.com/test/rich-results.

B3. Restore publishing cadence and close the content gap.
    posts.json shows 13 posts, but the dates run 2026-04-12 to
    2026-06-08 and then jump to 2026-09-04 — a three-month dead
    period where the workflow was broken (fixed in b243ac0).
    A blog whose last post is three months old reads as abandoned.
    Publish weekly for the four weeks before you reapply so the
    reviewer sees an active site. Verify the Mon/Wed/Fri cron in
    .github/workflows/publish-blog.yml actually produced a PR two
    consecutive cycles before you trust it again.

B4. Reduce ad density until you are approved.
    index.html currently carries 3 <ins class="adsbygoogle"> units
    against 849 words. Serving ads on 79-word tool pages is
    "ads on pages with little or no content" — a policy violation
    in its own right. Until B1 lands, either remove the ad units
    from the tool pages or leave them and finish B1 first. After
    B1, one ad above the fold and one mid-content is plenty.
    Never place an ad where it could be mistaken for a tool
    control ("Download" / "Convert") — accidental-click layouts
    are the fastest route to a permanent ban.

B5. Exclude non-content pages from the index. ALREADY DONE.
    CORRECTION to the first draft of this plan: login.html (37
    words), dashboard.html (129) and test-pwa.html all already
    carry <meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow"> and are
    already absent from sitemap.xml. The audit confirms it (G7
    passes). The original draft checked robots.txt but not the
    meta tags and called for work that is not needed.
    DO NOT add robots.txt Disallow entries for these pages. A
    disallowed page cannot be crawled, so Google never reads the
    noindex and the page can persist in the index. noindex and
    Disallow are alternatives, not a belt-and-braces pair, and
    noindex is already the correct choice here.
    Nothing to do for B5. It is retained in the numbering so the
    loop's gate map and this plan stay aligned.

B6. Verify the domain in Google Search Console.
    No google-site-verification meta tag exists on index.html and
    no verification file is present. Without GSC you are blind:
    no index-coverage data, no way to see whether the tool pages
    are even indexed, no manual-action notifications. Verify the
    domain, submit sitemap.xml, and confirm the tool pages appear
    under "Indexed" before reapplying.

B7. Make the site accountable to a human.
    AdSense reviewers look for a real publisher. You have
    about.html, contact.html, privacy-policy.html and
    terms-of-service.html — good. Two gaps:
      - contact.html still ships the Formspree placeholder
        YOUR_FORM_ID. A contact form that cannot receive a message
        is worse than no form. Swap in the real ID or replace it
        with the pdftojpgtool@gmail.com mailto link.
      - Blog posts carry no visible author. Add a byline and a
        two-line author bio linking to /about.html on every post,
        plus Article schema with a real author name. This is the
        cheapest E-E-A-T signal available to you.

B8. Confirm ads.txt is live and correct.
    Your ads.txt is right (pub-2042221703015293, DIRECT). Confirm
    it actually serves at https://www.pdf-to-jpg-tool.com/ads.txt
    and is not swallowed by the SPA fallback or the service worker.

2. PHASE 1 — TECHNICAL SEO FOUNDATION (week 2)
----------------------------------------------------------------
a) Sitemap lastmod: stamp real per-file dates from git history
   instead of "today" for all 39 URLs. Always-fresh lastmod values
   train Google to ignore the signal entirely.
b) Canonical tags: confirm every page self-canonicalises to the
   https://www. host. A canonical pointing at the apex domain while
   the CNAME serves www splits your signals.
c) Unique og:image per page. All pages currently share preview.jpg.
   1200x630, tool name + "free — never uploaded".
d) Visible breadcrumbs on tool and blog pages, matching the
   BreadcrumbList schema (same visible/marked-up rule as B2).
e) Service worker: sw.js must never serve a stale HTML shell to a
   crawler. Use network-first for HTML documents, cache-first only
   for static assets. A cached shell can serve Googlebot an empty
   page.
f) Core Web Vitals: tools.js is 395KB and tool-implementations.js
   is 152KB. Load per-tool bundles on demand rather than shipping
   all of it on every page. This is both a ranking factor and an
   ad-viewability factor (AdSense RPM drops when ads render late).
g) Internal linking: tool pages currently link nowhere. Every tool
   page should link to 3-4 sibling tools and 2-3 blog posts, and
   every blog post back to its primary tool. Pages-per-session is
   your RPM multiplier — it matters as much as rankings.

3. PHASE 2 — THE CONTENT ENGINE (weeks 2-10)
----------------------------------------------------------------
The auto-blog is the right machine; it needs the right queue. Keep
the human PR-review gate in publish-blog.yml — it is your guardrail
against both a repeat rejection and Google's Scaled Content Abuse
policy. Never move to un-reviewed daily auto-commit.

Also fix the queue data: scripts/topics.json relatedTools all point
at "/", and the generate-post.js prompt tells the model to use
href="/". Point them at the real tool URLs or the internal-linking
work in Phase 1 is undone on every new post.

Priority clusters, highest first:

PLATFORM LONG-TAILS (lowest competition, direct intent)
  - Convert PDF to JPG on iPhone (free, no app)
  - ... on Mac — 3 free ways
  - ... on Windows 11 without installing software
  - ... on Android / on a Chromebook

PRIVACY ANGLE (your only real differentiator)
  - Convert PDF to JPG without uploading your file anywhere
  - Is it safe to use online PDF converters? What to check first
  No competitor with a server-side pipeline can honestly claim
  this. Lead with it in every title, description and pitch.

QUALITY / SPEC LONG-TAILS
  - PDF to JPG at 300 DPI for print
  - Convert a multi-page PDF into separate JPGs (batch + ZIP)

MOFU LISTICLES (these attract backlinks)
  - "7 best free PDF to JPG converters compared (2026)" — include
    competitors honestly; win on the no-upload axis.

SIBLING CLUSTERS (faster wins, lift the whole domain)
  - Mirror the above for HEIC-to-JPG and the WebP tools.

Cadence: 2-3 posts/week, 1200+ words, every post linking to its
tool and to two sibling posts.

5. PHASE 4 — REAPPLY
----------------------------------------------------------------
Timing: after Phase 0 exits AND the site has run four weeks with
the new tool pages live, weekly posts, and GSC showing the tool
pages indexed. Roughly 5-6 weeks from today if Phase 0 starts now.

Before you click reapply:
  - Fetch 5 tool pages with JS disabled and read them as a
    stranger would. If any reads as a stub, you are not ready.
  - Run every template through the Rich Results Test.
  - Check GSC for manual actions. Fix any before applying.
  - Confirm ads.txt, privacy policy (must name Google, AdSense and
    cookie use), and a working contact route.

If rejected again, the reason string matters more than anything
else. "Low value content" = keep building content. "Site down or
unavailable" = crawl/robots/service-worker problem. "Valuable
inventory: no content" = the crawler saw the pre-JS shell, meaning
B1 did not actually land on the live site.

6. PHASE 5 — AFTER APPROVAL
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Ad placement: one unit above the fold, one mid-content, one at
  the end of long posts. Never adjacent to tool controls.
- Enable Auto ads only after you have manual-placement RPM data to
  compare against; Auto ads often place units badly on tool UIs.
- International (months 3-6, biggest untapped lever): conversion
  traffic is majority non-English and far less competitive.
  Translate the top 5 tool pages (Spanish, Portuguese, Indonesian,
  Hindi, French) as static pages with proper hreflang.
- Better networks are traffic-gated: Ezoic (~10k visits/mo, works
  with GitHub Pages via DNS), Monumetric (10k pageviews/mo),
  Mediavine (50k sessions/mo). These pay 2-5x AdSense RPM.
- Diversify (all GitHub-Pages compatible): affiliate links in the
  comparison and privacy posts (PDF software, privacy tools), a
  Ko-fi / Buy Me a Coffee button, and later a client-side "Pro"
  unlock via Gumroad or Lemon Squeezy. Details in seo-plan.txt §9.
- Never add crypto ad networks or anything that needs a backend.
